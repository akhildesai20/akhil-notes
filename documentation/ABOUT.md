# Akhil Notes

*The familiarity of handwriting. The convenience of typing.*

I've always found that writing something by hand helps me understand and remember it better. There is something about the deliberate movement of a pen, the rhythm of forming letters, and the freedom of an imperfect page that makes the process of thinking feel more personal.

But as more of my work moved to digital tools, handwritten notes gradually gave way to keyboards.

Akhil Notes began as a personal experiment to bring these two experiences a little closer together.

Rather than designing a typeface inspired by handwriting, I decided to turn my own handwriting into one. I started with pages from my notebooks, carefully translated individual characters into vector outlines, and gradually developed them into a functional typeface.

## Imperfect by nature

One of the most interesting discoveries during this process was how differently handwriting behaves when viewed in isolation and in context.

Individual characters often appear irregular. The curves are not always symmetrical, the proportions are not mathematically precise, and the strokes carry small inconsistencies. Yet when these same characters come together as words, sentences and paragraphs, they develop a surprisingly natural rhythm.

What appears imperfect in isolation becomes expressive in context.

Akhil Notes deliberately preserves these characteristics. Its personality comes from the way a person writes, rather than an attempt to reproduce handwriting through geometric precision.

## More than a handwritten typeface

While the project started with personal note-taking, its scope gradually expanded into something more versatile.

Handwriting fonts are often associated with greetings, invitations, illustrations and decorative typography. I wanted Akhil Notes to also be useful in situations where people work with information, organize thoughts, explain ideas and learn something new.

Its extended character set includes accented Latin letters, Vietnamese characters, mathematical operators, currency symbols, fractions, arrows, checkboxes and geometric symbols.

With six weights, matching italics and OpenType features, the typeface offers enough flexibility to establish hierarchy and emphasis while retaining a consistent handwritten personality.

## Designed for everyday expressions

Akhil Notes can find a place wherever digital communication could benefit from a more personal voice.

A student's study notes. A teacher's explanation of a mathematical concept. A designer's annotations on a prototype. A personal journal. A recipe passed down through generations. A checklist, a presentation, a financial planner or a simple reminder to oneself.

It is intended to be approachable enough for everyday use and versatile enough for more structured information.

## Personal origins. Shared possibilities.

What began as an attempt to preserve a familiar part of my own writing has evolved into an open-source typeface that anyone can make their own.

Akhil Notes is freely available under the SIL Open Font License, allowing individuals, educators, designers and developers to use it in personal and commercial projects.

The intention is not to replace handwriting, but to preserve a little of its humanity in the things we create digitally.
